# Mock Options Trading Games

Options market-making practice: a live **Simulation** and a put-call-parity **Drill**. No backend, no build step — plain HTML/CSS/JS.

## Running it

Easiest: double-click `index.html` to open it in your browser.

If your browser blocks local scripts from loading (some do), serve the folder instead:

```bash
python -m http.server 8000
```

then open `http://localhost:8000`.

## Delta-1 instrument

Both modes let you choose the **Future** (default) or the **Stock** as the delta-1 instrument.

- **Future:** no r/c, no buy-writes, no puts+stock, no combos. Options are priced undiscounted, so `C − P = F − K` exactly. The board is *Future* on top, then **Calls | Strikes | Puts | Straddles**.
- **Stock:** as before — r/c, **P+S | Calls | Strikes | Puts | B/W**, straddles and combos in the spread book.

## Simulation

- **1–5 strikes** (default 2). Call/put verticals sit in the gutters beside the calls and puts. The spread book (and IDB) also trade strangles, risk reversals (always a positive price, "calls over" when the call is richer and "puts over" when the put is — they have different payoffs), wide verticals, **butterflies** (3+ strikes) and **condors** (4+ strikes).
- **Requests are never tiny:** you're asked for a market in at least 20 lots on any instrument (20 to 200).
- **Fits any window:** the session screen (and the drills) are laid out for a window of about 1400 x 900 and, in anything smaller, or in a zoomed-in browser, are scaled down as a whole to fit — so nothing gets cut off.
- **Tick size:** 0.01, 0.05 or 0.10. Every price is on that tick, and the smaller the tick the tighter the markets.
- **Market widths follow vega × √lots:** a market's width is proportional to the instrument's vega times the square root of the volume — twice the vega is twice as wide, four times the lots is twice as wide (an ATM call is about 0.11 wide at 100 lots, 0.25 at 400; a wing call, with a quarter of the vega, is a quarter of that). The delta-1's width is decided as before. **Verticals are at most 0.05 wide** (0.10 on a 0.10 tick), whoever makes them.
- **Width requirement on your markets:** at most **1 wide** on any instrument, or **2 wide** when the size you've been asked for is more than **200 options or 100 straddles** (strangles, risk reversals and combos count as straddles). Verticals stay within their 0.05 / 0.10 cap whatever the size. Each request shows its limit ("max 1 wide"). **Within that, a market that's wider than it should be for its size just gets fewer trades** — from uninformed customers as much as informed ones (twice as wide is roughly a quarter as often), and an uninformed customer asking the IDB for a price passes more often on a wide one.
- **Order-book view** (toggle in config): the future/stock, calls, puts, straddles (and P+S / B/W) are drawn exactly like the drills' books — offers stacked on the right of a thick centre divider, bids on the left, a size bar growing out from the divider, square **Yours** / **Mine** buttons — with 2 levels a side and an empty level in the middle. Press **Mine** / **Yours** on the best price to take it (a ticket asks how many). To quote, type a price into the empty middle level's bid or offer box (at the best price or better), press Enter, then choose a size. A new best price rolls its own side's levels (bids roll down, offers roll up) and the lowest level drops off; trades and size changes don't animate.
- Anything without a full book (the call/put verticals and the spread book) shows just its top prices, `(x100) 10.50 - 10.60 (x150)`, with Yours / Mine underneath.
- **Trading mode** (config): **Timed** (events arrive on a clock, with a trade pace and a session length; the timer sits in the middle of the top bar) or **Step by step** (pick 10–100 steps, then press **Next step** — or space — to bring in each event). A step is one customer event: you're asked for a market, a customer improves a market, or a customer trades. **Taking a price yourself never uses a step**, and you can do as many of those as you like between steps. You can't move on while a market you've been asked for is waiting. The fair values drift one notch per step.
- **A session has two phases.**
  - **Setup (clock paused):** just enough markets are already trading to price the whole board and **no more** — a random mix of some verticals, some calls and some puts (with vol fixed, the ATM straddle is always one of them), plus the delta-1. You're then asked for **three markets** in the boxes nearest the money, one request at a time — each gets a big red border until you've made it — and that's it: the board isn't filled in up front, because the trading concentrates on a few strikes and a few instruments. The rest of the board fills in over the session as customers ask you for markets (mostly around the money). No customers trade until your three are made, and the clock (and so every fair value) holds still until then.
  - **Flow:** the customers arrive. They share a hidden **theme** — with a fixed delta-1 it's a view on vol (rising or falling vol, and the vol path really does move that way); with fixed vol it's a view on the market (rising or falling). Two kinds of customer:
    - **Informed** customers know where fair value is heading and **trade only when a price gives them an edge** against it, with the right tool for the view: **the delta-1 and in-the-money options for a view on direction, at-the-money options (and the straddle) for a view on vol** — nothing else. When one of them makes such a bet, the instruments that move with it **react**: buy ATM calls and the ATM puts and straddle get bid up; buy the future or in-the-money calls and the in-the-money puts get offered down.
    - **Uninformed** customers **always trade**, either way, at whatever the price is.
    - Customers take the whole best price about 70% of the time, otherwise part of it. After a trade, some put up **massive size on the same side** they just traded (a buyer bids, a seller offers), in the same market or a similar one.
  - The flow **concentrates on three strikes** — the at-the-money one and its neighbours — **and on a couple of the instruments** (say the calls and the straddles), and requests for a market come from the same place. In the spread book and the IDB, the structures asked about are built on what has traded most.
- **Customers never pull** their own prices. Prices only leave the book by trading (or by being pushed past the visible levels). When you or a customer trade a price out, that side goes **indicative** at the traded price until someone makes a new price there (and if a customer then bids through the old offer, there's simply no offer — only bids). Now and then a customer improves a price in size, sometimes against what parity says other markets are worth, so look for trades you can take.
- **Tighten a market and get a trade:** if you improve a two-sided market so that it's **75% or less as wide** as it was, a customer trades with you at your new price. Spot an arbitrage — say futures 99.95/100.05, calls 10.04/10.06, puts 9.95/10.05 — improve the put bid to 9.98, and you're bought; the conversion then locks in 0.01.
- **Strike column:** under each strike, the call's delta and the vega of one contract (the same units as the position panel).
- **Pull markets:** **Pull** removes your market from a book (or from just one side). Nothing is left in its place — no indicative price appears where your quote was.
- **Flow panel** (timed sessions): a log of everything that happens — red = a trade with you, yellow = a trade between others, blue = you took a price, green = a customer improved or posted a price. The panel and the market involved flash a coloured border for 3 seconds.
- **Trading History** (step by step; it replaces the Flow panel): every completed step, newest first and tagged #1, #2 ... (a request for a market counts as complete once you've made it), and everything you do between steps, tagged **You** — your trades, the markets you put in, the markets you pull. **Next step** turns blue when the step is complete and you can move on, and stays grey while a market you've been asked for is waiting. You can **improve as many markets as you like between steps**: every market (the book cells, the verticals and the spread book alike) has a bid and an offer box to quote into.
- **Position panel:** P&L at expiration (blue) and P&L if marked at today's Black-Scholes value (orange), plus total delta, gamma, vega and theta. The chart is centred on the strikes and reaches only 10 beyond the outermost ones on each side. Every trade — including customers trading against markets you made — is tracked.

## Drill

- **Order book view** (default) — a banner (score, timer), the question type centred beneath it, then a bold header per instrument ("95 Call") over a 3-level order book: offers stacked on the right of a thick centre divider (worst on top), bids on the left (best on top). Every level shows its size, e.g. `(x50)`, and a bar growing out from the divider in proportion to it (full at double the typical size: 200 for options and the underlying, 100 for straddles). **Yours** (sell the bid) and **Mine** (buy the offer) sit next to the divider on every level. Three types, in random order (on the drill config screen, *Arbitrage* and *Take liquidity* can each be switched off; *Make a market* is always on — in the single-price view there are no books to trade against, so the only question is working out a price):
  - **Every market shown has bid < ask** (and each deeper level is worse), and **nothing is ever bid for less than its intrinsic value** (or for nothing): call ≥ call − put, put ≥ put − call, straddle ≥ |call − put|.
  - *Make a Market*: only two other instruments' books are shown — enough to work the answer out — and the third is blank. Enter a two-way market: 1 point if the midpoint of the best market the shown books allow is inside yours (at most 1.00 wide), 2 if you have exactly the right bid and offer (an option's best bid is never below its intrinsic value, since you'd always pay that).
  - *Arbitrage Opportunity*: tick the best prices that make an arbitrage and press **Make Trades**, or press **No Arbitrage**. 1 point for any arbitrage, 2 for the best one (or for correctly spotting there is none).
  - *Take Liquidity*: some best prices are off fair value (a stray quote in front of an otherwise normal book) — take those only; trading anything else gives the edge away, and you stay on the question. Strays are only somewhat smaller than normal quotes, so size helps but isn't a giveaway (about half the single-leg questions can only be solved from prices). About 10% of questions have **nothing** worth taking (press *Nothing to Take*). About half come with a **print** in a blue box above the books — e.g. "Customer bought 1000 of the 120 Call for 1.80" — priced inside that instrument's market. Whether to believe it depends on two things: its **size** and its **instrument**. A **massive** clip (5-10x a typical quote) in an instrument someone would take a view with — the future or an in-the-money option (delta), an at-the-money option or straddle (vol) — is **informed**: its price is that instrument's fair value and settles ties between ways of lining the books up. A massive clip in an out-of-the-money option has no reason behind it, so it's just a price inside the market and can mislead. About 75% of prints are massive (a fifth of those in uninformative instruments); the other 25% are only about the size of the books and are noise too. 1 point for a right leg, 2 for all the right legs (or for correctly calling nothing). A best price that is one leg of a **riskless package** in the books (e.g. the straddle bid, when the call and put offers cost less) also scores 1 — it can be hedged for a locked-in profit, even though it isn't the best trade (which builds a bigger position, with more edge but more risk).
  - You always take a price's entire size, and each price is independent. Only best prices count toward an arbitrage or a right leg; deeper levels are depth, and ticking one is wrong. Wrong answers clear your picks and keep the question.
- **Explain Last Answer:** from the second question on, a button on the right edge pauses the timer and re-draws the last question with the best trade highlighted on the books, plus an explanation of why it was the best — the replication behind a market, the arbitrage and why it's riskless, the fair values behind a liquidity trade, and how to read the print. Close it to restart the timer.
- **Single price view:** one price is missing — work it out and type it.

The drills are on a $0.05 tick.

## End-of-session review

When a simulation ends you get a **score out of 10** and feedback. **Put-call parity is the main thing** — it carries 4.5 of the 10: 3.0 for how well your markets line up with what the other books at the strike imply (a bid above the implied value, or an offer below it, can be traded against for a risk-free profit), and 1.5 for spotting parity gaps and arbitrages on the screen rather than walking past them. The other 5.5: a reasonable edge in your markets (1.0), staying vega neutral (1.0) and delta neutral (1.0) — credit for hedging, especially by improving other markets to get filled in the right direction — plus-EV trades (1.0), trying to be top of book (0.5), and making money (1.0). It then walks through a moment you did well and one you could have done better, with the numbers. It's a judgement read off what you did, so the risk and profit scores especially are approximate.
